# Pump and PumpSwap parity

P6. The bonding-curve and pool math, checked against the router rather than
against itself. A quoter that agrees with nothing external is an expensive way
to restate your own assumptions.

Generated by `pnpm pump:parity` into `artifacts/pump-parity.json`.

## What is checked

Our independently derived curve output against Jupiter's routed output, for the
same mint at the same instant, at several sizes.

The curve is decoded from the account, not from documentation:

```
discriminator   17b7f83760d8ac60   (8 bytes)
verified layout 49 bytes
tail            extensible; 115 and 151 both observed live
virtual offsets 30 SOL / 279.9e12 tokens
```

The tail matters. Two different account lengths were seen in production; a
decoder that assumed one length would have silently misread the other, and a
misread reserve produces a plausible price rather than an error.

## Reading it

**123 bps, stable across three sizes on the same mint.** A residual that does
not move with size is not a math error in the curve — a wrong constant-product
formula diverges as size grows, and this does not. It is a fixed wedge, which is
what a router fee plus its own slippage allowance looks like.

**The router never beat the curve.** Zero of four. If our curve were
overstating output we would expect the router to win sometimes; it never does,
which is consistent with the residual being the router's take rather than our
error.

**257 bps on the other mint** is inside the spread and is one observation. Four
comparisons is not a parity proof, and it is not presented as one.

**Four were skipped because the curve moved between the two reads.** Those are
correctly discarded rather than compared: a curve that changed between our read
and the router's is two different markets, and the difference would be time, not
math.

## What this does not establish

- **Not a simulation parity result.** This compares two *quotes*. Whether a
  Pump transaction executes with the effect its quote described is
  `SIMULATED_EFFECT_OK`, and no Pump fingerprint has reached it yet. See
  `docs/CAPABILITY_MATRIX.md`.
- **PumpSwap canonical pools are not covered here.** The observed corpus is
  overwhelmingly `Pump.fun Amm` (4,545 observations) with 22 on `Pump.fun`
  proper. The canonical-pool path needs its own comparison before it can carry
  an evidence class.
- **Four rows.** Sufficient to show the residual is structural rather than
  size-dependent. Insufficient for anything else.

## Why Pump first

Pump is where the opportunity actually is in this corpus: of 26,515
observations, `Pump.fun Amm` accounts for 4,545 and every other venue together
for fewer than 60. Proving the simulator against a stable pair through an AMM
the strategy never trades tells us nothing about whether the strategy can be
measured.

A Pump-only valid development window may begin while every other venue remains
structural-only.
